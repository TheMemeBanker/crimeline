The core principle

You trade the crowd, never a house.

CrimeLine is not a sportsbook. There is no bookmaker setting odds against you and no house bankroll on the other side of your trade — just another player who reads the city differently.

How a match works

When you take over, your order sits in the market until a player takes under at the same price. Matched stakes form the pot; the settlement number decides who takes it. Prices you see — like "61% over" — are the crowd's live consensus, not odds we set.

What CrimeLine does — and never does

We do: post the lines, match orders, run the live data feed, and settle every market from the official number. For that, the protocol takes a small fee on winnings — the fee schedule is published in the rulebook.

We never: take the other side of your trade, hold your funds in a house account, set odds against you, or touch a settled result. If nobody takes the opposite side of your order, it simply doesn't match — and an unmatched order costs you nothing.

Why you can trust the number

It's public record. Settlement data is what city police departments publish — anyone can pull the same records and check.

The provider is independent and licensed. CrimeLine cannot add, remove or edit a report, and the provider is contracted and publicly named in the rulebook before the first market opens.

Everyone sees the same feed. No premium tier, no early looks — one snapshot for every player and every market.

We have no side. The protocol earns a fee on winnings whichever way the number lands — there is nothing for us to gain from over or under.

And it's all checkable. Every settlement number is sealed on Solana; if the feed ever fails, markets void and stakes refund in full.

Questions

Asked & answered.

Is this betting on crime happening to people?
No. CrimeLine markets trade on aggregate, publicly-reported city statistics — the same totals cities publish in open-data portals and year-end reports. No market ever references an individual, an address, or a specific event, and nothing on CrimeLine affects what happens in a city. It's the same relationship a weather market has to rain.
Which wallets are supported?
Phantom, Solflare and Backpack connect today — on desktop and in their mobile in-app browsers. If you don't have one, the connect dialog links you to the official downloads. Your wallet stays non-custodial: CrimeLine can see your public address and nothing else.
Do I need SOL if everything is in USDC?
A little. Positions and payouts are in USDC, but Solana network fees are paid in SOL — typically fractions of a cent per transaction. A few dollars of SOL covers months of trading.
When do markets go live?
After the devnet beta and an independent audit. The sequence is: devnet beta (now) → audited mainnet deployment → first cohort boards on the ten launch cities. Connected wallets are first in line. Follow @CrimeLineMarket for the countdown.
What happens if the data feed goes down?
Markets have explicit void-and-refund rules — if the licensed feed can't produce a settlement number for a city by the deadline, that city's markets void and stakes return in full. The exact finality, void and tie rules are in the rulebook.
How does CrimeLine make money if there's no house?
A small fee on winnings — charged only when you win a matched market, never on deposits, unmatched orders or refunds. The protocol earns from volume, not from your losses, so it has no stake in which side wins.
Who can play?
When markets open: 18+, subject to eligibility, identity verification and applicable law, and not available where prohibited. The beta preview and wallet linking are open to everyone.
